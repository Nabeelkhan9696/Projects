Crystal cat litter — often sold as "clear" or "silica gel" litter — has quietly
become one of the fastest-growing categories in the pet aisle. But what actually
makes it different from the clumping clay most of us grew up with?

## How crystal litter works

Crystal litter is made from **silica gel**, the same porous material you find in
those little "do not eat" packets. Each granule is riddled with microscopic pores
that trap moisture and lock away odor-causing ammonia.

Instead of clumping around urine, the crystals **absorb the liquid and let it
evaporate**, leaving the waste behind to dry out. You scoop solids daily and stir
the crystals so they absorb evenly.

## The main benefits

- **Superior odor control.** Silica traps ammonia far more effectively than most
  clay litters.
- **Very low tracking.** The granules are large and lightweight, so less litter
  ends up scattered across your floor.
- **Less frequent changes.** A single fill can last a single cat 3–4 weeks.
- **Lightweight bags.** Easier to carry home and pour than heavy clay.

## The trade-offs

- **Higher upfront cost** per bag (though the longer lifespan narrows the gap).
- Some cats **dislike the texture** underfoot and need a slow transition.
- Crystals **don't clump**, so a multi-cat box can look "used" faster.

## Who it's best for

Crystal litter shines for **single-cat households**, people sensitive to dust,
and anyone who wants to scoop less often. If you have multiple cats or a kitten
that might nibble granules, transition carefully and watch the box closely.

> Bottom line: clear crystal litter is a genuinely strong choice for odor and
> convenience — just budget for the texture adjustment period.
