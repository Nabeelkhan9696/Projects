Litter tracking — those granules that hitch a ride on paws and scatter across the
floor — is one of the most common cat-owner complaints. Here's how to beat it.

## 1. Switch to a low-tracking litter

Larger, heavier granules (like crystal or premium clumping formulas) simply travel
less than fine, dusty litters.

## 2. Add a litter mat

A textured or honeycomb mat outside the box catches granules as your cat steps
out. Empty it into the box every few days.

## 3. Use a high-sided or top-entry box

Higher walls keep kicked litter contained. Top-entry boxes force paws to shed
granules through the lid grate before your cat hops down.

## 4. Keep the litter depth right

Two to three inches is the sweet spot. **Too deep** and cats kick more out;
**too shallow** and they dig to the bottom.

## 5. Trim paw fluff

For long-haired cats, gently trimming the fur between the paw pads gives granules
fewer places to hide.

## 6. Place the box strategically

A corner or low-traffic nook with a mat "runway" leading out gives litter room to
fall off before it reaches your living space.

## 7. Vacuum little and often

A quick daily pass around the box stops small amounts of tracked litter from
spreading further into the home.

> Stack two or three of these together and most people see tracking drop by more
> than half within a week.
