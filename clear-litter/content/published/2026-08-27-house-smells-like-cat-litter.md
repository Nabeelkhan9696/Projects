<p>If you can smell the litter box from the hallway, something in the system is failing &mdash; and it's almost never "cats just smell." A litter setup that's working traps odor at the source. The good news: the usual causes are fixable in about 15 minutes, and keeping the smell gone afterward takes minutes a day, not an overhaul.</p>

<h2>Why does my house smell like cat litter?</h2>
<p>Because urine odor is escaping faster than your litter can trap it &mdash; usually for one of three reasons: the litter is saturated and has stopped absorbing, the box sits in a dead-air spot that concentrates smell, or odor has already soaked into surfaces <em>around</em> the box (mats, baseboards, grout) so cleaning the box alone doesn't help.</p>
<p>The smell itself is mostly ammonia, produced as bacteria break down urine. That matters for the fix: ammonia keeps being produced as long as liquid urine is sitting somewhere &mdash; in the bottom of a clay box, in a urine-soaked mat, or in litter past its capacity. Masking it with air freshener does nothing; removing the liquid does everything.</p>

<h2>The 15-minute fix</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Minutes 0&ndash;3: empty the evidence.</strong> Scoop all solids and, if you use clumping clay, dig out every clump &mdash; including the flat ones stuck to the bottom. If the litter smells of ammonia even after scooping, it's saturated: dump all of it.</li>
<li><strong>Minutes 3&ndash;6: wash the box.</strong> Hot water and unscented dish soap, then dry it fully. Skip bleach and strong-smelling cleaners &mdash; residual chemical smell can put cats off the box entirely.</li>
<li><strong>Minutes 6&ndash;10: treat the surroundings.</strong> Wash or replace the litter mat, and wipe the floor and baseboards within a metre of the box with an enzyme cleaner. This is the step most people skip, and it's where "mystery" smell usually lives.</li>
<li><strong>Minutes 10&ndash;12: refill correctly.</strong> Two to three inches of fresh litter &mdash; deep enough to absorb, shallow enough that it gets stirred by use.</li>
<li><strong>Minutes 12&ndash;15: move the air.</strong> Open a window or run the bathroom fan for a few minutes. Odor that has nowhere to go settles into fabric and paint.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Is your litter itself the problem?</h2>
<p>Often, yes. Clumping clay controls odor by trapping urine in clumps &mdash; which works until a clump breaks, smears on the pan, or sits longer than a day. Every missed clump is a little ammonia factory.</p>

<h2>Where you put the box matters more than you think</h2>
<p>A perfect box in a closed closet will still smell, because ammonia concentrates in still air. Quick placement checks:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Airflow beats hiding.</strong> A corner with some air movement beats a sealed cabinet. If the box must live in a closet or cabinet, leave the door cracked and add a vent.</li>
<li><strong>Not next to soft surfaces.</strong> Rugs, curtains, and sofas absorb odor and re-release it. Hard floors wipe clean.</li>
<li><strong>One box per cat, plus one</strong> in multi-cat homes &mdash; overloaded boxes saturate mid-week no matter what litter you use. Our <a href="/blogs/news/crystal-litter-multiple-cats">multi-cat guide</a> covers the full setup.</li>
</ul>

<h2>When the smell is a warning sign</h2>
<p>A litter box that suddenly smells much stronger &mdash; without a change in litter or routine &mdash; is worth paying attention to. Stronger ammonia, more frequent urination, or urine that smells sweet or unusually pungent can be early signs of a urinary tract infection, kidney issues, or diabetes in cats. If the smell changed and your cat's box habits changed too, call your vet.</p>

<h2>FAQ</h2>
<p><strong>Why does my house still smell after cleaning the litter box?</strong><br>
Because the odor is no longer in the box. Urine soaks into litter mats, grout, baseboards, and nearby fabric, and keeps releasing ammonia after the box is spotless. Clean a one-metre radius around the box with an enzyme cleaner and wash or replace the mat.</p>
<p><strong>Does crystal litter really smell less than clay?</strong><br>
For urine odor, yes &mdash; crystals dehydrate urine instead of holding it as damp clumps, so bacteria have no liquid to convert to ammonia. Solid waste still needs daily scooping with any litter; no product removes that job.</p>
<p><strong>How often should I change litter to stop the smell?</strong><br>
Scoop solids daily with any litter. Clumping clay typically needs a full change weekly to fortnightly; silica crystal litter runs about four weeks per cat with daily stirring. If you can smell ammonia when you walk in, change it now regardless of schedule.</p>
<p><strong>Is a smelly litter box harmful?</strong><br>
Mild odor is a maintenance signal, not a hazard, but concentrated ammonia in unventilated spaces irritates airways &mdash; yours and your cat's. A sudden unexplained change in urine smell is also worth a vet call, since it can signal a UTI, kidney trouble, or diabetes.</p>
