Here's the straight answer: **an 8 lb bag of crystal cat litter lasts about
3–4 weeks for one cat**, with daily solid scooping and a quick stir of the
crystals. That single number is why people switch — but it moves around a lot
depending on how many cats you have and how you maintain the box. Here are
the real numbers.

## How long does an 8 lb bag actually last?

| Household | Realistic lifespan of one 8 lb bag |
| --- | --- |
| 1 cat | 3–4 weeks |
| 2 cats (sharing boxes) | ~2 weeks |
| 3+ cats | 1–1.5 weeks |
| 1 kitten or senior cat (more frequent urination) | 2–3 weeks |

Two caveats worth knowing. First, these assume you **scoop solids daily and
stir the crystals** so moisture spreads to fresh beads — a neglected box
saturates faster. Second, cats vary: a cat on wet food urinates more than a
dry-food cat, and some medical conditions increase urination. Treat your
first bag as a calibration month.

## Why does crystal litter last longer than clay?

With clumping clay, every urination removes litter from the box — you scoop
the clump, the level drops, you top it off. You're constantly consuming
litter.

Crystal litter works differently: the silica beads **absorb urine into
themselves and let the water evaporate**, so nothing leaves the box except
the solids you scoop. The same beads keep working for weeks until they
reach saturation. That's how one 8 lb bag does the work of roughly four
bags of clay — and why the bag you carry home is so much lighter. (Full
breakdown in our
[crystal vs. clumping comparison](/blogs/news/crystal-vs-clumping-cat-litter-which-is-actually-better).)

## How do you know when it's time for a full change?

The crystals tell you. Replace the whole bag when you see any of these:

- **Most beads have changed color** (yellowed/cloudy) — they're saturated.
- **Odor comes back** even right after scooping — saturated crystals can't
  absorb fresh ammonia.
- **The litter feels damp** when you stir instead of dry and loose.
- **Liquid pools** at the bottom of the pan — past due; change it now.

When any of those show up, dump the whole box, wipe the pan, and refill
with a fresh 2 inches. Don't mix old saturated crystals into a new bag —
you'll drag the odor problem forward.

## How can you make a bag last longer?

1. **Stir daily.** This is the big one. Stirring when you scoop distributes
   moisture across all the beads instead of soaking one wet spot.
2. **Keep depth right (about 2 inches).** Too shallow and urine hits the
   pan; too deep and you're just using more litter than the month needs.
3. **One box per cat, plus one.** Boxes saturate per-use — spreading cats
   across boxes stretches every bag.
4. **Scoop solids daily.** Crystals handle liquid; solids are on you, and
   leaving them accelerates odor regardless of litter quality.
5. **Place the box somewhere dry.** Humid bathrooms make silica work
   overtime absorbing air moisture instead of urine.

## FAQ

**Does stirring really extend the life of crystal litter?**
Yes — it's the single most effective habit. Urine soaks the beads where it
lands; stirring spreads moisture to dry beads so the whole bag reaches
saturation together instead of one corner failing early.

**Can I just add new crystals on top of old ones?**
Top-ups work mid-cycle if the level drops, but once most beads have changed
color, do a full change. Mixing fresh crystals into saturated ones brings
the odor along.

**How long does crystal litter last for two cats sharing one box?**
About two weeks, sometimes less. You'll get better litter economics (and
happier cats) with two boxes — each lasts close to the full 3–4 weeks
because usage is split.
