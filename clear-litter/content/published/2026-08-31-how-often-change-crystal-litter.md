<p>Crystal litter's biggest selling point &mdash; weeks between changes &mdash; is also where most new users go wrong, in both directions. Some dump a perfectly good box at day ten out of clay habit; others stretch one bag past six weeks and wonder why the odor control "stopped working." Here's the schedule that actually fits how the crystals work, and how to read the box instead of the calendar.</p>

<h2>How often should you change crystal cat litter?</h2>
<p>For one cat, do a full change every <strong>3&ndash;4 weeks</strong>, with solids scooped daily and a quick stir of the crystals so urine reaches fresh beads. Two cats sharing a box roughly halves that to about two weeks &mdash; though two cats really should have two boxes, which puts each box back on the monthly rhythm.</p>
<p>The reason the schedule is measured in weeks rather than days: silica beads absorb urine and let the moisture evaporate, holding the odor-causing compounds inside the bead. The bed keeps working until enough beads are saturated &mdash; then it stops fairly abruptly, which is why the calendar is a guide and the signs below are the truth.</p>

<h2>The full maintenance rhythm</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Daily (under a minute):</strong> scoop solids, then stir the crystals. The stir is what most people skip, and it's half the lifespan &mdash; unstirred boxes saturate one corner while the rest of the bag stays fresh.</li>
<li><strong>Weekly:</strong> wipe the rim, check the mat, and eyeball the overall bead color.</li>
<li><strong>Every 3&ndash;4 weeks:</strong> dump everything, wash the box with hot water and unscented soap, dry it, and refill 2&ndash;3 inches deep.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What are the signs the litter is spent?</h2>
<p>Change the box when you see any of these, whatever the calendar says:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Most beads have turned yellow.</strong> Scattered yellow patches are normal working litter; when the whole bed is tinted, capacity is gone.</li>
<li><strong>Ammonia smell returns</strong> within a day of scooping. Saturated beads can't hold new urine, so it sits and does what urine does. (If the box smells bad but the beads still look fresh, it's usually a cleaning or placement issue &mdash; our <a href="/blogs/news/house-smells-like-cat-litter">litter smell guide</a> walks through that diagnosis.)</li>
<li><strong>Damp patches or pooling.</strong> Liquid sitting on top of the bed is the clearest possible "done" signal.</li>
<li><strong>More fines and dust</strong> than usual when stirring &mdash; weeks of digging grinds beads down, and an old bed sheds more. (More on that in our <a href="/blogs/news/crystal-litter-dust">dust explainer</a>.)</li>
</ul>

<h2>What changes the schedule?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>More cats, more throughput.</strong> The bed's capacity is fixed; two cats fill it twice as fast.</li>
<li><strong>Health and diet.</strong> Cats on wet food, kidney-support diets, or medications that increase drinking produce noticeably more urine. Senior cats often drink more, too &mdash; and a <em>sudden</em> jump in output without a diet change is worth a vet conversation, not just more litter.</li>
<li><strong>Humidity.</strong> Silica also pulls moisture from the air; a box in a steamy bathroom loses some capacity to the room itself. Drier placement, longer life.</li>
<li><strong>Bed depth.</strong> A shallow bed saturates fast. The 2&ndash;3 inch fill isn't marketing &mdash; it's the capacity math.</li>
</ul>

<p>A note on the difference between this schedule and the bag's advertised lifespan: "lasts up to a month" assumes one average cat, a full-depth bed, and daily stirring. Real households drift from all three, which is why we published <a href="/blogs/news/how-long-does-crystal-cat-litter-last">real-world longevity numbers</a> separately &mdash; if your bags are consistently dying early, that guide helps you work out which variable is eating the difference before you conclude the litter is at fault.</p>

<h2>FAQ</h2>
<p><strong>Can I just stir crystal litter instead of changing it?</strong><br>
Stirring extends the bed's life by spreading urine to unused beads, but it doesn't add capacity. Once most beads have yellowed or smell returns quickly after scooping, stirring is finished helping — do the full change.</p>
<p><strong>What happens if I don't change crystal litter for too long?</strong><br>
Saturated beads stop absorbing, urine pools, ammonia builds, and many cats start refusing the box — which usually ends with accidents elsewhere. Past the four-week mark for one cat, you're trading a few dollars of litter for those risks.</p>
<p><strong>Do I need to wash the box at every change?</strong><br>
Yes, a quick one — hot water and unscented dish soap, then a full dry before refilling. Residue on the pan floor is where lingering smells hide, and a damp pan wastes the new bed's capacity from minute one.</p>
<p><strong>Can I mix new crystals into old litter?</strong><br>
It works poorly. New beads mixed into a spent bed absorb everything themselves and the box smells again within days. Spot-removing the most saturated patch mid-cycle is fine; mixing as a change-avoidance strategy costs more litter than it saves.</p>
