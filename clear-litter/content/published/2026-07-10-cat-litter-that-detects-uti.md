Cats are famously good at hiding illness. By the time a urinary tract
infection is obvious — straining in the box, crying, going outside the box —
it has often been brewing for days or weeks. **Health-monitoring cat litter
gives you a window into your cat's health you'd otherwise never see: the
crystals change color when urine chemistry looks abnormal.**

Here's how it works, what the colors mean, and who should be using it.

## How can a litter detect a UTI?

Silica crystal litter absorbs urine directly into the bead instead of
clumping around it. That property makes something clever possible:
indicators built into the crystals that react to what's *in* the urine.

Our [health monitoring litter](/products/health-monitoring-cat-litter) uses
color-changing crystals that respond to two of the most useful early
signals:

- **pH shifts.** Abnormally alkaline or acidic urine is associated with
  urinary tract infections, bladder crystals, and stones.
- **Traces of blood.** Often invisible to the naked eye in litter, blood in
  urine is an early flag for UTIs, cystitis, and kidney problems.

You don't have to do anything differently — your cat uses the box as normal,
and you glance at the crystal color when you scoop.

## What do the color changes mean?

The rule of thumb is simple: **a consistent, unusual color change is a
reason to call your vet — not a diagnosis.** Think of it like a smoke alarm:
it doesn't tell you what's burning, it tells you to look now, while the
problem is still small.

A one-off odd patch after a diet change or new treats can be harmless. A
color change that repeats over a day or two, especially with any behavior
change (more frequent box visits, smaller amounts, licking, hiding), is
worth a vet visit. Bring a photo of the litter — vets find it genuinely
useful context.

## Why does early detection matter so much for cats?

Urinary problems are among the most common — and most dangerous — feline
health issues:

- **UTIs and cystitis** cause pain long before obvious symptoms, and cats
  instinctively mask that pain.
- **Urinary blockages**, especially in male cats, can turn fatal within
  24–48 hours. They almost always start with subtler urinary changes.
- **Chronic kidney disease** affects a large share of senior cats, and by
  the time appetite and weight visibly change, significant function may be
  gone.

The economics matter too: a routine vet visit prompted by an early warning
costs a fraction of an emergency blockage surgery — and is a far better day
for your cat.

## How to use it well

1. **Fill to about two inches** and stir the crystals when you scoop solids
   daily — even absorption keeps the indicators readable.
2. **Check color at every scoop.** It takes two seconds once it's habit.
3. **Know your baseline.** After a few days you'll know what "normal" looks
   like for your cat, which makes anomalies obvious.
4. **Replace every 3–4 weeks** for one cat, sooner in multi-cat homes.
5. **On repeated color changes, call your vet** and mention what you saw
   and when. Early information is the whole point.

## FAQ

**Can litter really diagnose a UTI?**
No — and be wary of anything that claims to. Health-monitoring litter flags
urine chemistry changes (pH shifts, traces of blood) that warrant a vet
visit. Diagnosis needs a urinalysis at the clinic.

**Does it work in a multi-cat household?**
Yes, but you'll know *a* cat has an issue, not which one. Use one box per
cat (plus one spare) to narrow it down, or temporarily separate cats if you
see repeated color changes.
