The golden rule of changing cat litter: **cats accept new litter when the
change is gradual, and reject it when it's sudden.** Swap everything
overnight and even an easygoing cat may decide your laundry basket is the
better bathroom. Mix the new litter in over about a week, and most cats
barely notice the transition happening.

Here's the exact 7-day plan we recommend, plus what to do if your cat is
the stubborn type.

## Why can't I just swap litters overnight?

Cats form strong preferences about litter **texture** — it's what their
paws know. Crystal beads feel completely different from fine clay sand,
and a box that suddenly feels wrong can trigger box avoidance: going next
to the box, on rugs, or in quiet corners. Once a cat picks an alternative
spot, breaking the habit is much harder than preventing it.

A gradual mix lets the paws adjust while the box still smells and mostly
feels familiar. That's the whole trick.

## The 7-day transition schedule

Start with a clean box and your normal clay at the usual depth, then:

- **Days 1–3: 25% crystals, 75% old litter.** Mix gently so crystals are
  distributed, not sitting in a layer on top. Scoop as normal.
- **Days 4–6: 50/50 mix.** Most cats show no reaction at this stage — a
  good sign to keep going.
- **Days 7–9: 75% crystals, 25% old litter.** The box now behaves mostly
  like crystal litter: stir it when you scoop solids.
- **Day 10: 100% crystals.** Full change: empty the box, wipe the pan dry,
  and fill with about 2 inches of fresh crystals.

Yes, "7 days" is really 7–10 — go by your cat, not the calendar. If every
stage passes without hesitation, you can compress it; many cats are fully
switched inside a week.

## What if my cat hesitates?

Watch for the warning signs: sniffing the box and walking away, perching
on the edge instead of stepping in, or going less often. If you see them:

1. **Slow down.** Drop back to the previous ratio for 3–4 more days.
   There's no prize for finishing fast.
2. **Try the two-box method.** Put 100% crystal litter in a second box
   next to the original clay box and let your cat choose. Curiosity wins
   surprisingly often — and when your cat starts using the crystal box
   voluntarily, you're done.
3. **Keep everything else identical.** Same box, same location, same
   scooping routine. Change one variable at a time — the litter.

## Mistakes that sabotage the switch

- **Changing litter and box location at the same time.** Two changes at
  once doubles the chance of rejection.
- **Switching during other upheaval** — moving house, a new pet, a diet
  change. Wait for a calm stretch.
- **Overfilling the box.** Crystals work best at about 2 inches; a deep
  box feels unstable underfoot.
- **Punishing accidents.** If your cat goes outside the box, that's
  feedback about the pace, not misbehavior. Clean thoroughly, slow the
  transition down, and add the second box.
- **Quitting at the first hesitation.** Most "my cat hates crystal
  litter" stories are really "we went too fast" stories.

## FAQ

**How long does it take a cat to get used to crystal litter?**
Most cats fully adjust within 7–10 days using a gradual mix. Confident
cats may switch in under a week; cautious ones can take two to three.

**Can kittens use crystal litter?**
Wait until at least 8 weeks old — younger kittens explore by chewing, and
a non-clumping paper or clay litter is safer until they're past that
phase. After 8 weeks, transition them the same gradual way.

**What if my cat eats a crystal?**
A nibbled bead or two passes through harmlessly — silica gel is
non-toxic. If a cat repeatedly eats litter of any kind, that can signal a
dietary issue worth mentioning to your vet.

**My cat peed outside the box mid-switch. Do I give up?**
No — slow down. Return to the previous mixing ratio, clean the accident
spot with an enzyme cleaner so it doesn't become a habit, and consider
the two-box method so your cat opts in at their own pace.
